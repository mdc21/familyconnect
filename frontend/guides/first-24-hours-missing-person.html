The first 24 hours when someone is missing

When a relative or group member cannot be contacted after a sudden Himalayan flash flood, taking clear, organized actions in the first 24 hours makes tracing far more effective.

Hour-by-Hour Action Plan

Follow this checklist to document essential details, alert emergency authorities, and avoid common errors.

  1. Hours 1–2: Check primary channels & last known contact

    Try calling SMS, WhatsApp, satellite phones, or social media messaging. Check if their trekking partners, guide, or tour operator have checked in. Record the last timestamp and cell tower location if known.

  2. Hours 2–4: Write down a comprehensive descriptor profile

    Document exact details: full legal name, passport or citizenship number, age, height, clothing worn on the day, backpack color, medical conditions, scars/tattoos, and photos taken right before departure.

  3. Hours 4–8: Register the case on FamilyConnect

    Submit a report on FamilyConnect to generate an official case tracking reference (`FC-NP-2026-XXXXX`). Store the access code securely. This reference coordinates across Nepal Police and humanitarian assistance desks.

  4. Hours 8–12: Notify statutory authorities and embassy

    Report to local police, district administration (NDRRMA), and the relevant embassy/consulate if the person is a foreign national. Provide your FamilyConnect case reference so records link automatically.

  5. Hours 12–24: Appoint a Proxy Contact if needed & check Assistance Hubs

    If you are outside Nepal or travelling between facilities, delegate a trusted local proxy on FamilyConnect to receive updates on your behalf. Visit or call Family Assistance Centres in Kathmandu or Rasuwagadhi.
