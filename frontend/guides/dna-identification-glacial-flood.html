DNA identification: a guide for families

Where recovered remains cannot be identified visually due to mudslide trauma, comparative DNA analysis provides the scientific basis for formal identification.

Privacy & Safeguarding Notice

FamilyConnect never holds or stores genetic sequence data. We record only your contact details, relationship claim, and laboratory paperwork reference numbers.

  1. 1. Who should provide a reference sample?

    First-degree biological relatives (mother, father, biological child, or full sibling) yield the highest statistical probability for genetic matching. Second-degree relatives (grandparent, half-sibling) can also assist when first-degree relatives are unavailable.

  2. 2. How to apply for a DNA sample kit

    Fill out the Request a DNA sample kit form on FamilyConnect. You will receive an immediate `DNA-YYYYMMDD-XXXXX` tracking reference. A coordinator will arrange an appointment at a designated forensic clinic or hospital.

  3. 3. Giving the sample

    The sample is taken using a painless buccal (cheek) swab by trained medical staff. You will be issued a laboratory tracking barcode.

  4. 4. How comparisons are performed and confirmed

    The accredited forensic laboratory compares your reference sample against profiles obtained from remains. A human forensic pathologist reviews every match candidate. Only the responsible statutory authority (Nepal Police / District Coroner) can issue a formal identification certificate.
