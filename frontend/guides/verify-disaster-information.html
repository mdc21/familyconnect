How to check whether disaster information is verified

During a major flash flood emergency, rumors and false predictions circulate rapidly on social media. Understanding how to evaluate information prevents panic and ensures families act on ground facts.

Verification Levels Explained

FamilyConnect strictly labels every piece of news with source attribution and verification status. An unverified update is not a confirmation.

  1. VERIFIED / CONFIRMED

    Information verified directly by official authorities (NDRRMA, Nepal Police, Red Cross, Ministry of Health) or satellite sensing data. You can rely on this for decision-making.

  2. UNVERIFIED

    Reports gathered from media outlets or eye-witnesses that are currently under review by ground coordinators. They provide early awareness but must not be treated as confirmed fact.

  3. FALSE / DEBUNKED

    Claims that have been checked against official hydrological data, authorities, or field teams and found to be untrue (e.g. false dam collapse warnings or precise flood hour predictions).

  4. MISLEADING / PARTIAL FACT

    Messages that contain a element of truth but exaggerate scope or misrepresent dates, locations, or casualties.
